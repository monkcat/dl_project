\begin{abstract}
We propose using the typed element graph of an academic paper as a
\emph{single source of truth} for retrieval. The same edge-weight schema
(i)~defines a graded relevance distribution for a listwise contrastive
loss (\textbf{GRCL}), strictly generalizing binary InfoNCE, and
(ii)~constructs a transition matrix for plug-in inference-time score
propagation. On SPIQA test-A, GRCL improves R@10 by $+2.7$pp and MRR by
$+5.4$pp ($p\!=\!0.042$). Most strikingly, applying the same graph
propagation to the external GME-Qwen2-VL-2B MLLM raises its R@10 from
$58.3$ to $\mathbf{84.4}$ ($+26$pp) and MRR from $33.1$ to
$\mathbf{63.3}$ ($+30$pp) \emph{without any fine-tuning}---the study's
largest single effect.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

The answer to a query on an academic paper is usually a \emph{set} of related
elements rather than a single one: a figure, its caption, the citing
paragraph, and an appendix reference, linked through typed relations like
\texttt{caption\_of} and \texttt{refer\_to}. Existing systems do not treat
this graph as a first-class signal---text-centric RAG drops figures,
page-level visual RAG~\cite{colpali,visrag} can miss cross-page evidence,
and element-level retrieval without graph priors ranks each candidate
independently.

We propose using the document's typed element graph as a \emph{single
source of truth}, with one edge-weight schema driving two independent
contributions. \textbf{(C1) Graph-Relevance Contrastive Loss (GRCL):} a
2-hop max-product path kernel defines a graded relevance distribution over
candidates; a listwise cross-entropy fits the retriever to it. GRCL
strictly generalizes binary InfoNCE. \textbf{(C2) Plug-in graph
propagation:} the \emph{same} edge weights construct a transition matrix
used by weighted diffusion or Personalized PageRank to smooth any base
retriever's scores; no retraining is required. Empirically, applying~(C2)
to the external retrieval-tuned MLLM GME-Qwen2-VL-2B~\cite{gme} raises its
SPIQA R@10 from $58.3$ to $84.4$ ($+26$pp) and almost doubles its MRR
($33.1\!\to\!63.3$), evidence that the graph functions as an
\emph{encoder-agnostic universal prior}.


\section{Method}

\paragraph{Element graph schema.}
Each document is a per-document graph $G_d\!=\!(V_d,E_d)$ with six node
types (\texttt{text}, \texttt{figure}, \texttt{table}, \texttt{caption},
\texttt{equation}, \texttt{section\_header}) and five typed edges. Edges
are extracted directly from arXiv LaTeX source: \texttt{caption\_of} from
$\backslash$caption proximity to the nearest $\backslash$includegraphics or
\texttt{tabular}; \texttt{refer\_to} from $\backslash$ref macros bound to
their referent label; \texttt{contains} from $\backslash$section nesting;
and \texttt{reading\_next}/\texttt{section\_next} from sequential order
within and across sections.
The framework's \emph{single source of truth} is the edge-weight schema
$\text{BASE}\!=\!\{\texttt{caption\_of}\!:\!1.0,\,
\texttt{refer\_to}\!:\!0.8,\,\texttt{contains}\!:\!0.5,\,
\texttt{reading\_next}\!:\!0.3,\,\texttt{section\_next}\!:\!0.2\}$.
A section-role modifier (appendix$:\!1.3$, references$:\!0.5$) emphasizes
appendix evidence and de-emphasizes citation lists; a cross-modal
visual-target modifier ($1.1$ for \texttt{refer\_to} into a figure/table)
slightly favors paragraph-to-visual references over paragraph-to-paragraph
ones.

\paragraph{Encoder.}
SigLIPv2-base-patch16-224~\cite{siglip2} with LoRA rank~8 on attention
projections (\texttt{q\_proj}, \texttt{k\_proj}, \texttt{v\_proj},
\texttt{out\_proj}) of both towers; the pretrained backbone is frozen,
so only $\sim$491k LoRA + projection-head parameters are updated
($0.32\%$ of the base). Token-level outputs are projected to
$D_\text{proj}\!=\!128$ and L2-normalized. The retrieval score uses
ColBERT-style MaxSim~\cite{colbert}:
$\mathrm{score}(q,e)=\sum_i\max_j\langle q_i,e_j\rangle$. Token-level
late interaction is well-suited to academic figures: a single salient
phrase in the query can match a single discriminative patch of the
figure, which is harder to capture with single-vector cosine
similarity.

\paragraph{Graph-Relevance Contrastive Loss (GRCL).}
The kernel between anchor $q$ and candidate $e$ is the max-product path
weight up to two hops with decay $\gamma\!=\!0.5$:
\begin{equation}
g(q,e)\;=\;\max_{\pi:q\to e,\,|\pi|\le 2}\ \prod_{(u,v)\in\pi} w_{uv}\,\gamma^{|\pi|-1},
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $w_{uv}$ uses BASE and target-modifier corrections. For a candidate
pool of $K\!=\!12$, let $r_i\!=\!g(q,e_i)$ and $s_i\!=\!\mathrm{score}(q,e_i)$.
The loss is a soft-target listwise cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{GRCL}(q)\;=\;-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{r_i}{\sum_j r_j}\,\log\frac{\exp(s_i/\tau)}{\sum_j\exp(s_j/\tau)}.
\label{eq:grcl}
\end{equation}
When the targets degenerate to $\{0,1\}$ this recovers binary InfoNCE;
GRCL is therefore a strict graded relaxation. The anchor is sampled
uniformly from three kinds---a \texttt{caption\_of} pair (visual anchor),
a \texttt{refer\_to} pair (body anchor), and a natural-language QA
question---so a single retriever learns to handle all three query
distributions encountered at evaluation time. The candidate pool of
$K\!=\!12$ mixes graph 1-hop positives, same-document negatives (which
share section context but lie outside the relevance subgraph), and
cross-document negatives (drawn from other papers in the batch),
preventing the loss from collapsing to a within-document shortcut.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:grcl} shows the full training step.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{GRCL training step (one anchor)}
\label{alg:grcl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require document $d$ with graph $G_d$, encoder $f_\theta$, temperature $\tau$, decay $\gamma$
\State sample anchor $q$ from $\{\texttt{caption\_of},\texttt{refer\_to},\texttt{nl\_qa}\}$ with prob.\ $1/3$ each
\State build pool $C\!=\!\{e_1,\ldots,e_{12}\}$: 1-hop positives $\cup$ same-doc negatives $\cup$ cross-doc negatives
\For{$i = 1,\ldots,K$}
  \State $r_i \gets g(q, e_i)$  \Comment{eq.~(\ref{eq:kernel}); 2-hop max-product BFS on $G_d$}
  \State $s_i \gets \mathrm{MaxSim}(f_\theta(q),\, f_\theta(e_i))$  \Comment{ColBERT-style late interaction}
\EndFor
\State $\mathcal{L} \gets -\sum_i (r_i / \textstyle\sum_j r_j)\log\!\bigl[\,\mathrm{softmax}(s/\tau)\bigr]_i$  \Comment{eq.~(\ref{eq:grcl})}
\State update $\theta$ (LoRA adapter $+$ projection head) via AdamW
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Inference-time graph propagation.}
Given any base retriever's scores over $V_d$, we propagate on $G_d$ to
re-rank the top-$N\!=\!50$ candidates---a truncation that keeps the
$50\!\times\!50$ matrix-vector iteration trivial ($<\!1$\,ms per query)
while preserving recall, since SPIQA's per-document pools rarely exceed
$N$ relevant elements. The transition matrix $P$ is the row-normalized
weight matrix from the \emph{same} schema as the training kernel; $P$ is
built once per document at eval time. We support three regimes
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:prop}): (A)~\emph{uniform diffusion} with all edge
weights set to one (structure-only), (B)~\emph{weighted diffusion}, and
(C)~\emph{Personalized PageRank} with teleport vector
$q\!=\!s^{(0)}$~\cite{ppr}. All three are eval-only and apply to any base
retriever without parameter updates---including external retrieval-tuned
MLLMs that have no notion of our edge schema (the schema lives entirely
on the propagation side).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Inference-time graph propagation (per query)}
\label{alg:prop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require base scores $s\in\mathbb{R}^{|V_d|}$, graph $G_d$, regime $\in\{\text{uniform},\text{weighted},\text{ppr}\}$, $\alpha$, $T$
\State $\mathcal{T} \gets$ top-50 indices of $s$;\quad restrict $G_d$ and $s$ to $\mathcal{T}$
\State build $P\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{50\times 50}$: row-normalize edge weights (or all-ones if uniform)
\State $s^{(0)} \gets s$;\quad $q \gets s$ \Comment{$q$: PPR teleport}
\For{$t = 1,\ldots,T$}
  \If{regime $\in \{\text{uniform},\text{weighted}\}$}
    \State $s^{(t)} \gets (1\!-\!\alpha)\,s^{(t-1)} + \alpha\,P^\top s^{(t-1)}$  \Comment{diffusion}
  \Else
    \State $s^{(t)} \gets \alpha\,q + (1\!-\!\alpha)\,P^\top s^{(t-1)}$  \Comment{PPR; iterate until $\|s^{(t)}\!-\!s^{(t-1)}\|_\infty<10^{-4}$ or $T_\text{max}\!=\!30$}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathcal{T}$ re-ranked by $s^{(T)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\section{Experiment}

\paragraph{Setup.}
SPIQA~\cite{spiqa} is a figure-grounded QA benchmark over arXiv CS papers.
We train on the \texttt{train\_val} split (25{,}459 papers, $\sim$250k
caption--figure pairs) and evaluate on \texttt{test-A} (118 papers,
999 queries). Graphs are extracted directly from arXiv LaTeX source
($\backslash$section, $\backslash$ref) and the dataset's
\texttt{all\_figures} JSON, so no PDF parsing is needed. Training uses
AdamW for $8{,}000$ steps with $16$ anchors $\times\,12$ candidates per
step; the default setting is $\text{lr}\!=\!3$e-5, $\tau\!=\!0.07$,
$\gamma\!=\!0.5$, and a tuned setting is $\text{lr}\!=\!1$e-4, $\tau\!=\!0.10$.
One row takes $\sim$5~h on a single A100 80GB. Evaluation uses a
per-document candidate pool, reporting R@5, R@10, and MRR; significance is
assessed by paired bootstrap ($1{,}000$ resamples). Baselines are (a) the
same encoder trained with binary InfoNCE, and (k=GME) the external
retrieval-tuned MLLM GME-Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct~\cite{gme}, zero-shot.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} summarizes our findings.
\textbf{(F1) GRCL outperforms InfoNCE} (rows 1 vs.~2): $+2.7$pp R@10 and
$+5.4$pp MRR; paired bootstrap on Coverage@10 gives
$\Delta\!=\!+0.0255$, $95\%$ CI $[+0.0015,+0.0511]$, $p\!=\!0.042$. The
relative MRR gain ($+16.5\%$) is $\sim$$5\times$ the relative R@10 gain,
indicating that graded supervision sharpens top-rank quality.
\textbf{(F2) The \texttt{refer\_to} edge dominates the graph signal}
(row~3): restricting the kernel to \texttt{refer\_to} alone yields R@10
$=\mathbf{90.4}$, the best of all 43 ablation rows. Explicit body--figure
references carry the strongest training signal; trivial adjacencies
(\texttt{caption\_of}) and structural containment (\texttt{contains}) are
weaker. We attribute this to information density: a $\backslash$ref macro
is a manual evidence annotation by the paper's author, whereas
\texttt{caption\_of} is a trivial layout co-occurrence that adds little
beyond cosine similarity. Mixing weak edges into the kernel
\emph{dilutes} the strong signal---using all five edges loses $3.5$pp
R@10 over the \texttt{refer\_to}-only kernel.
\textbf{(F3) Hyperparameter tuning is a comparable lever} (rows 4--5):
switching $\text{lr}$ alone gives $+1.7$pp R@10 / $+5.2$pp MRR; the full
combo reaches R@10 $88.0$ / MRR $42.2$.
\textbf{(F4) Plug-in propagation is the largest single gain}
(row~7 vs.~8--12): applying the same schema on top of GME-Qwen2-VL-2B
raises R@10 from $58.3$ to $84.4$ ($+26$pp) and MRR from $33.1$ to
$63.3$ ($+30$pp), \emph{without any fine-tuning}. GME$+$propagation
reaches $96\%$ of our best trained SigLIP's R@10---demonstrating that
the graph schema is encoder-agnostic. The phenomenon matches classical
label-propagation theory~\cite{labelprop}: when the base retriever's
initial scores are noisy with respect to the true relevance
distribution, smoothing along an informative graph closes most of the
gap to a strong supervised model, while a well-fit base retriever
gains little from further smoothing. Practically, this means our
propagation step is a deployable inference-time module that can be
attached to commercial embedding services, sparing the
$\sim$$100$~GPU-hour cost of training a per-corpus retriever.
\textbf{(F5) Three propagation regimes are equivalent on GME}
(rows~8--12): uniform diffusion, weighted diffusion, and PPR all converge
to R@10 $83$--$85\%$ and MRR $59$--$63\%$, indicating that propagation
utility comes from \emph{graph structure} itself rather than from
modifier sophistication.

\section{Conclusion}

A typed document element graph can simultaneously serve as graded
supervision for retriever training and as an inference-time prior for any
base retriever, driven by a single edge-weight schema. On SPIQA, GRCL
gives a statistically significant gain over binary InfoNCE,
\texttt{refer\_to}-only edges yield the best trained R@10 ($90.4$), and
plug-in propagation atop the external GME-Qwen2-VL-2B MLLM produces our
largest single effect ($+26$pp R@10, $+30$pp MRR). Limitations: evaluation
is restricted to one in-domain benchmark; cross-domain generalization and
learnable edge-type weights are clear next steps.
